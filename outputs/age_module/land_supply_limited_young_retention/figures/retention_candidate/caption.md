# English caption

***Fig. 4 | Protecting young secondary forests increases pre-peak carbon uptake and lowers peak warming alongside wood and land trade-offs.***

*a, Young-forest protection scenarios in which land conversion and standing harvest proposed in secondary forests younger than a prescribed age threshold (10, 20, 30 or 40 years) are cancelled without displacing demand onto older forests. b, Annual change in forest area by stand age class under the 40-year protection threshold relative to the unprotected age-dependent baseline in the Very Low (VL) and Low-to-Negative (LN) pathways. Horizontal dashed lines mark the 40-year threshold, and text labels report total additional forest area in 2099 and the portion aged 40 years or older. c, Additional cumulative net land carbon uptake over 2023–2099 plotted against the remaining difference in peak warming relative to the age-invariant reference. Faint points show individual ensemble members, open symbols show ensemble medians across the four protection thresholds, and filled circles with vertical bars show the median and 5th–95th percentile range of the unprotected baseline on an inverse-hyperbolic-sine (asinh) vertical scale. d, Ensemble-median reductions over 2023–2099 in above-ground carbon delivered by secondary-forest standing harvest (left, with horizontal bars showing 5th–95th percentile ranges), gross non-forest land-use transitions not executed (centre), and gross new forest establishment included within those foregone transitions (right). Gross transition areas sum annual events and can count the same land parcel more than once (n = 500 parameter sets per pathway).*
