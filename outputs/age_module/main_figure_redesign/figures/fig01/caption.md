***Fig. 1 | Lagged carbon uptake in new forests raises peak warming and delays temperature return to 1.5 °C.***

*a, Paired future simulations branching from a shared 2022 state under identical emissions, land-use and harvest drivers, comparing age-dependent production (age on) against an age-invariant reference (age off). b, Ensemble-median global surface warming relative to 1850–1900 in the Very Low (VL) and Low-to-Negative (LN) pathways over 2022–2099. c, Warming in 2099 under age on versus age off across ensemble members. Shading marks members at or below 1.5 °C under age off but above 1.5 °C under age on, red filled circles mark members that lose their pre-2100 return to 1.5 °C, and the inset magnifies the region around 1.5 °C. d, Proportions of the ensemble that never exceed 1.5 °C, exceed 1.5 °C and return before 2100, or remain above 1.5 °C in 2099, with ribbons showing transitions between age off and age on. Lost-return percentages are expressed relative to members that return under age off (n = 190 in VL and 138 in LN). e, Joint distribution of the paired change in peak warming over 2022–2099 (positive in 99.6% of VL and 97.6% of LN members) and additional years spent above 1.5 °C over 2023–2099 (median change 0 years in both pathways). Colour shading shows the percentage of members per bin on a logarithmic scale, the horizontal axis uses an inverse-hyperbolic-sine (asinh) scale, and marginal histograms show the univariate distributions (n = 500 parameter sets per pathway).*
